# Kinematics — Speed, Velocity, and Acceleration

Lesson 3 of 4

---

## Objectives

Distinguish average from constant speed and velocity, and describe acceleration as speeding up, slowing down, or changing direction.

---

## Review: Speed and Velocity

- **Speed** = how fast (scalar), $s = \dfrac{d}{t}$
- **Velocity** = how fast and which way (vector), $v = \dfrac{\Delta x}{t}$
- Speed is the **rate of distance traveled**; velocity is the **rate of change of position**.
- Units of speed and velocity: $\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}$.

---

## Average Speed
- Average speed is total distance dividied by total time.

- $s_{average}=\frac{d_{total}}{t_{total}}$

---

## Average Speed

A van travels **120 m east** in 10 s, then **80 m east** in 5 s, then **100 m west** in 5 s. What is its average speed?

<v-clicks>

- $d_{total} = 120\ \mathrm{m} + 80\ \mathrm{m} + 100\ \mathrm{m} = 300\ \mathrm{m}$
- $t_{total} = 10\ \mathrm{s} + 5\ \mathrm{s} + 5\ \mathrm{s} = 20\ \mathrm{s}$
- $s_{average}=?$
- $s_{\text{average}} = \frac{d_{\text{total}}}{t_{\text{total}}} = \frac{300\ \mathrm{m}}{20\ \mathrm{s}} = 15\ \frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}$
- $\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}$, scalar

</v-clicks>

---

## Average Velocity

- Average velocity is total displacement dividied by total time.
- $v_{average}=\frac{\Delta x_{total}}{t_{total}}$

---
zoom: 0.90
---

## Average Velocity

A van travels **120 m east** in 10 s, then **80 m east** in 5 s, then **100 m west** in 5 s. What is its average velocity?

<v-clicks>

- $\Delta x_{total} = 120\ \mathrm{m}+80\ \mathrm{m}-100\ \mathrm{m} = 100\ \mathrm{m\ east}$
- $t_{total} = 20\ \mathrm{s}$
- $v_{average}=?$
- $v_{average} = \frac{\Delta x_{\text{total}}}{t_{\text{total}}} = \frac{100\ \mathrm{m}}{20\ \mathrm{s}} = 5\ \frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}\ \text{east}$
- $\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}$, east

</v-clicks>

---

## Constant Speed and Constant Velocity

<v-clicks>

- **Constant speed**: the rate of distance traveled never changes.

- **Constant velocity**: the **magnitude and direction** of velocity never change. This only happens when moving in a **straight line** at a **constant speed**.

- A constant speed but changing direction is **not** constant velocity.

</v-clicks>

---

## What is Acceleration?

- **Acceleration** is the **rate at which velocity changes**.
- An object accelerates when it:
  - **speeds up**,
  - **slows down**, or
  - **changes direction**.
- Acceleration is a **vector**.




---

## Acceleration Equation and Units

$$a = \frac{\Delta v}{t} = \frac{v - v_0}{t}$$

- $v_0$ = initial velocity, $v$ = final velocity, $t$ = time.
- $\Delta v = v - v_0$ is the **change in velocity**.

$$[a] = \frac{\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}}{\mathrm{s}} = \frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}^2}$$

- Units: $\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}^2}$ — read "meters per second squared."


---

## Summary

- **Average speed** uses total **distance**; **average velocity** uses total **displacement**.
- **Constant** means never changes; **average** is for the whole trip.
- An object accelerates when it **speeds up**, **slows down**, or **changes direction**.
- **Acceleration**: $a = \dfrac{v - v_0}{t}$
