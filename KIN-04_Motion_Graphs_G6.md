# Kinematics — Motion Graphs

Lesson 4 of 4

---

## Learning Objective

Students will be able to read a position-time graph: find the position at a given time, describe the motion from the shape of the line, and find velocity from the slope.

---

## Review: Acceleration

- **Acceleration** is the rate at which velocity changes:

$$a = \dfrac{v - v_0}{t}$$

- Units: $\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}^2}$.
- Same direction as velocity→ speeding up
- Opposite direction as velocity→ slowing down.

---

## Motion Graphs

The motion of an object can be shown on a graph with **time** on the $x$-axis:

- **Position-time graph** — how position changes with time

---

## Position-Time Graphs

<img src="/x-t_graphs.excalidraw.svg" width="720" />

- The $y$-value tells you the **position** at a given time.
- The slope of the line tells you the **velocity**.

---

## Velocity from a Position-Time Graph

On a **position-time** graph, the **slope** is the velocity.

$$v = \dfrac{\Delta x}{t} = \dfrac{x - x_0}{t} \textcolor{#888888}{\left( \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} \right)}$$

<v-clicks>

- A steeper slope means a **greater velocity**.
- Positive slope: $v\rightarrow$ or $v\uparrow$
- Negative slope: $v\leftarrow$ or $v\downarrow$

</v-clicks>

## Summary

- A **position-time** graph shows position on the $y$-axis and time on the $x$-axis.
- The **slope** of the line is the **velocity**.
- A **flat** line means the object is **at rest**.
- A **steeper** slope means a **greater velocity**.
