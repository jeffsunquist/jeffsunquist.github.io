## The Friction Equation Template

> [!IMPORTANT]
> $F_f = \mu F_N$ is the **template** for friction — not a third type. Fill it in for the situation:
>
> **Sliding:** $F_k = \mu_k F_N$
>
> **At rest:** $F_s \le \mu_s F_N$, with maximum $F_{s,\max} = \mu_s F_N$

- Use $F_k$ and $\mu_k$ when the object is **sliding**.
- Use $F_s$ and $\mu_s$ when the object is **not moving**.
- $F_N$ is the normal force; on a flat surface with only weight pressing down, $F_N = mg$.
- **Static friction is stronger:** $\mu_s > \mu_k$, so $F_{s,\max} > F_k$ at the same $F_N$.
- Rearrange to find the normal force: $F_N = \dfrac{F_k}{\mu_k}$ or $F_N = \dfrac{F_{s,\max}}{\mu_s}$.

| Surface pair | $\mu_s$ | $\mu_k$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| rubber on dry concrete | $0.9$ | $0.8$ |
| wood on wood | $0.5$ | $0.4$ |
| steel on ice | $0.1$ | $0.05$ |
