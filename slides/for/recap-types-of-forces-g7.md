## Review: Types of Forces

- Forces are either **contact** (the objects touch) or **non-contact** (they act at a distance).
- **Weight** $F_g$ is the non-contact force: $F_g = mg$.
- Contact forces: the **normal force** (perpendicular to a surface), **tension** (along a rope), and **friction** (opposes motion or attempted motion).
- $F_T$ and $F_N$ have **no formula of their own** — they are given or solved from the free-body diagram, using $F_{net}=0$ or $F_{net}=ma$. In equilibrium, $F_T = F_g$ and $F_N = F_g$.
- **Friction:** $F_f = \mu F_N$ is the template. Use $F_k = \mu_k F_N$ when sliding, or $F_s \le \mu_s F_N$ when at rest (maximum $F_{s,\max} = \mu_s F_N$).
- **Static friction** ($F_s$) holds a still object; **kinetic friction** ($F_k$) opposes sliding.
- **Static is stronger:** $\mu_s > \mu_k$.
- Static friction is only as large as needed: $F_s \le \mu_s F_N$.
